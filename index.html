Hao Lu

Robotics research · Planning & manipulation

School of Computing, Australian National University · Canberra, Australia

I am a robotics researcher and systems engineer working on reliable robot planning and manipulation at the Australian National University (ANU). My research builds on more than a decade at Freescale/NXP, where I worked on embedded systems, software/hardware co-design, heterogeneous AI inference, performance optimization, and customer-facing deployment.

My research connects planning, optimization, learning, and simulation with robotic systems. I study motion planning under uncertainty, task and motion planning, nonprehensile manipulation and rearrangement, and decision-making in partially observed environments. I build research systems with C++ and Python, ROS 2, MoveIt 2, OMPL, Isaac Sim, and mathematical optimization.

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science (Robotics) at ANU, supervised by Rahul Shome, Hanna Kurniawati, and Marco Cheung. I received my Master of Machine Learning and Computer Vision from ANU and was a graduate exchange student at the University of Toronto. I welcome conversations about robotics research, engineering, and collaboration.

Manipulation of Tabletop Cuboids in Dense Clutter with Parallel Grippers: Complexity and Methods

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome (Hao Lu: corresponding author)

ICRA 2027 · Under review

Dense cuboid arrangements can block the continuous range of grasps available to a parallel gripper. This work reduces grasp feasibility to a finite set of geometric witnesses and represents the resulting dependencies as a Horn system. It characterizes the complexity of selecting clearance actions and develops a mixed-integer planning formulation, evaluated in physics simulation and closed-loop real-robot trials.

Conference & workshop papers

Contact-Rich Slide–Pick Manipulation Planning for Dense Cuboid Clutter with Parallel Grippers

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome (Hao Lu: corresponding author)

CoRL 2026 workshop “Open Problems in Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation” · Accepted

Paper / PDF

Parallel grippers can be blocked by neighboring objects even when a cuboid would be graspable in isolation. This work combines directional slides with continuously parameterized picks, reducing grasp feasibility to a finite dependency system. A mixed-integer formulation minimizes the slides needed to clear the scene. Simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments examine how geometric planning and perception feedback support execution in dense clutter.

Virtues of Ordered Chaos: Planning with Topple Actions in Tabletop Stack Rearrangement

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome

IROS 2026 · Accepted

Paper / PDF / Code

Rearranging a stack by picking one object at a time can require many intermediate moves. This work introduces a graph abstraction for toppling, allowing a planner to interleave actions that move several objects with conventional pick-and-place. A partially grounded task-and-motion framework resolves uncertain outcomes during execution. Isaac Sim experiments investigate when these richer interactions reduce execution time.

Zero-Shot Generative World Priors for Planning and Object Reaching in Partially Observed Rooms

Hao Lu, Subhransu S. Bhattacharjee, Dylan Campbell, Rahul Shome

RSS 2026 workshop “Robot Planning in the Era of Foundation Models” · Accepted

Paper / PDF

A robot reaching for an object often sees only part of its surroundings. This work uses off-the-shelf generative vision models to sample plausible unseen room structure without task-specific fine-tuning. The resulting hypotheses provide a distribution of semantically labeled 3D worlds for planning, allowing decisions to account for multiple possible environments instead of relying on a single reconstructed scene.

Optimal Knock-Pick Planning for Tightly Packed Tabletop Blocks With Parallel Grippers

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome

WAFR 2026 · Accepted

Paper / PDF / Code

In tightly packed grids, blocks can lack the clearance needed for a parallel-gripper pick. This work introduces directional knocks that expose graspable objects and formulates the choice of knocks through a graph abstraction. A reduction to maximum-weight perfect matching gives a polynomial-time optimal plan under the studied assumptions. Synthetic and Isaac Sim experiments examine scalability and execution of the resulting knock-pick sequences.

Selected for an invitation to submit an extended version to IJRR.

Sampling-based Motion Planning for Optimal Probability of Collision under Environment Uncertainty

Hao Lu, Hanna Kurniawati, Rahul Shome

IROS 2024

Paper / PDF

When obstacle locations are uncertain, commonly used path-cost approximations can misrepresent the probability of collision. This work formulates collision probability across sampled environments and combines roadmap search with mixed-integer linear optimization. It establishes asymptotic properties for the probabilistic objective and evaluates the approach on a seven-degree-of-freedom arm, showing why the choice of risk formulation matters for planning.

Arbitor: A Numerically Accurate Hardware Emulation Tool for DNN Accelerators

Chenhao Jiang, Anand Jayarajan, Hao Lu, Gennady Pekhimenko

USENIX ATC 2023

Paper / PDF / Code

Hardware optimizations can alter the numerical behavior and accuracy of neural networks. Arbitor uses machine-learning compiler infrastructure to emulate accelerator arithmetic on general-purpose GPUs, supporting rapid evaluation before hardware is built. The study examines low-precision arithmetic, approximate computing, and structured sparsity across several DNN families, connecting accelerator design choices to their effects on model accuracy.
