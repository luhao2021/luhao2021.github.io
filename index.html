Hao Lu

Robotics research · Planning & manipulation

School of Computing, Australian National University · Canberra, Australia

I am a robotics researcher and systems engineer at the Australian National University (ANU), working on reliable robot planning and manipulation. I bring more than a decade of experience at Freescale and NXP, where I developed embedded and edge-computing systems, optimized AI inference and software performance, and supported deployment with customers.

My research combines planning, optimization, and learning to help robots act reliably under uncertainty. I focus on task and motion planning, manipulation and rearrangement, and decision-making in partially observed environments. My work connects algorithmic analysis with simulation and real robotic systems, using C++ and Python, ROS 2, MoveIt 2, OMPL, Isaac Sim, and mathematical optimization.

I am pursuing a PhD in Computer Science (Robotics) at ANU, supervised by Rahul Shome, Hanna Kurniawati, and Marco Cheung. I hold a Master of Machine Learning and Computer Vision from ANU and completed a graduate exchange in Computer Science at the University of Toronto. I welcome research collaborations and opportunities to develop capable, reliable robotic systems.

Contact-Rich Slide–Pick Manipulation Planning for Dense Cuboid Clutter with Parallel Grippers

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome Hao Lu is the corresponding author.

CoRL 2026 workshop “Open Problems in Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation” · Accepted

Paper / PDF

Dense clutter can leave a parallel gripper without enough clearance to grasp an object. We combine directional slides with continuously parameterized picks, representing grasp constraints as a finite dependency system. A mixed-integer formulation finds a plan that minimizes the slides needed to clear the scene. Simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments evaluate the approach with perception feedback during execution.

Virtues of Ordered Chaos: Planning with Topple Actions in Tabletop Stack Rearrangement

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome

IROS 2026 · Accepted

Paper / PDF / Code

Rearranging stacks one object at a time can require many intermediate moves. We introduce a graph abstraction for toppling that lets a planner combine actions affecting several objects with conventional pick-and-place. A partially grounded task-and-motion framework resolves uncertain outcomes during execution. Experiments in Isaac Sim evaluate how these richer interactions can shorten execution while exposing the challenges of uncertain contact outcomes.

Zero-Shot Generative World Priors for Planning and Object Reaching in Partially Observed Rooms

Hao Lu, Subhransu S. Bhattacharjee, Dylan Campbell, Rahul Shome

RSS 2026 workshop “Robot Planning in the Era of Foundation Models” · Accepted

Paper / PDF

A robot reaching for an object may observe only a small part of the room. We use off-the-shelf generative vision models to sample plausible completions of the unseen space, without task-specific fine-tuning. These hypotheses form a distribution of semantically labeled 3D worlds. Planning across this distribution accounts for alternative environments instead of committing to a single reconstruction.

Optimal Knock-Pick Planning for Tightly Packed Tabletop Blocks With Parallel Grippers

Hao Lu, Rahul Shome

WAFR 2026 · Accepted

Paper / PDF / Code

Tightly packed blocks can lack the lateral clearance required by a parallel gripper. We introduce directional knocks that expose graspable objects and formulate the choice of knocks through a graph abstraction. A reduction to maximum-weight perfect matching yields an optimal plan in polynomial time under the grid assumptions studied. Synthetic benchmarks and Isaac Sim experiments evaluate the method’s scalability and execution.

Selected for an invitation to submit an extended version to IJRR.

Sampling-based Motion Planning for Optimal Probability of Collision under Environment Uncertainty

Hao Lu, Hanna Kurniawati, Rahul Shome

IROS 2024

Paper / PDF

Uncertain obstacle locations make collision risk a property of the whole path, which common cost approximations can misrepresent. We formulate collision probability across sampled environments and combine roadmap search with mixed-integer linear optimization. The method provides asymptotic guarantees for the probabilistic objective. Experiments with a seven-degree-of-freedom arm demonstrate the importance of modeling risk consistently when selecting a path.

Arbitor: A Numerically Accurate Hardware Emulation Tool for DNN Accelerators

Chenhao Jiang, Anand Jayarajan, Hao Lu, Gennady Pekhimenko

USENIX ATC 2023

Paper / PDF / Code

Hardware optimizations can change the numerical behavior and accuracy of neural networks. Arbitor uses machine-learning compiler infrastructure to emulate accelerator arithmetic on general-purpose GPUs, enabling evaluation before hardware is built. We study low-precision arithmetic, approximate computing, and structured sparsity across several DNN families, relating accelerator design choices to their effects on model accuracy.
